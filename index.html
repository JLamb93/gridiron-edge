How these projections are built +

Every number on this page is computed in your browser from a data snapshot researched against public sources (ESPN game logs, team injury reports, and public betting-market references) as of the "Updated" timestamp above — there's no hidden model and no outside call at page load. A production version would automate that same research each morning from free public sources (ESPN's public scoreboard/roster endpoints, the nflverse play-by-play archive, and team injury reports) and recompute on that schedule — nothing here depends on a paid odds feed. Lines marked ~ are pace-based estimates because a firm Week 7 number wasn't posted at research time, not a live quote.

1. Blended baseline

50% last-3-game average, 30% full-season average, 20% most recent game — recent form counts, but one outlier game can't swing it.

2. Matchup adjustment

Shifts the baseline up to ±12% based on the opponent's rank against that position — a bottom-5 defense nudges the projection up, a top-5 defense pulls it down.

3. Availability discount

Questionable/probable tags shave the projection (7% / 2%) for expected snap or target share loss. A player ruled Out is flagged and skipped.

4. Probability, not certainty

Game-to-game variance (from a real log, or a generic estimate when one isn't on file) sets a spread around the projection; that spread estimates the odds of clearing the line — shown as a percentage, never a lock.

5. Two tiers of depth

13 "featured" players carry a full 5-game log and researched market lines. Everyone else — the rest of the league's starting QBs and top skill players — runs on season-pace averages only, clearly labeled "Season snapshot," with the line set to that pace rather than a sourced market number.

6. Why QB passing charts are mostly blank

Next Gen Stats' free public pages don't publish a per-player field-side or depth-of-target breakdown — that level of detail is behind NFL's paid data products. Where NGS does publish a real aggregate (avg. intended air yards, aggressiveness, time to throw), it's shown; otherwise the panel says so rather than estimating a chart.

This is an analytical estimate, not a guarantee.
